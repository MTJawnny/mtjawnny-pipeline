## 1. Card Resource Delta — fixture contract

Card Resource Delta (CRD) remains a **derived accounting fact**, not a canonical family. Its accounting unit remains a distinct underlying card-origin resource. A spell/card copy, generated token, mana unit, or repeated execution opportunity is not automatically another CRD unit.

The existing candidate needs one bounded clarification for stateful cards:

1. Every CRD result MUST name a `baseline_observation_state`.
2. Every CRD result MUST name an `observation_state_or_window` for the after-state being compared.
3. The accounting SHOULD preserve `resource_identity_set_before` and `resource_identity_set_after`, or an equivalent identity-preserving transition list.
4. `identity_turnover` MUST remain visible when quantity delta is zero.
5. `pending_acquisition` MUST be distinct from a currently accessible resource.
6. Intermediate states MAY be retained as `intermediate_deltas`; they MUST NOT be silently summed into a final durable delta when later events reverse them.

These are accounting coordinates. They do not create another family and do not require present-tense affordability to define resource identity.

### CRD-1 — consumed draw spell

Synthetic shape: a one-card spell is cast from hand and resolves by drawing two cards; assume no relevant graveyard-access permission.

- before: source card is accessible in hand;
- transition: source is consumed from the hand-access set;
- transition: two newly drawn card identities enter the hand-access set;
- after: `-1 + 2 = +1` for the caster under this explicit baseline/window.

**Required lesson:** the same `draw two` consequence generated by a retained permanent or trigger has different source-consumption accounting. The draw count alone is not a CRD fact.

### CRD-2 — targeted discard

Synthetic Mind-Rot-like shape: source spell leaves the caster's accessible hand set and two target-player hand identities leave that player's accessible hand set.

- caster: source-consumption delta is preserved;
- target: two resource identities are lost;
- multiplayer result: retain the per-player vector and pairwise comparisons where needed.

**Required lesson:** do not collapse a multiplayer resource event into one opaque scalar centered on the source controller.

### CRD-3 — draw then discard / identity turnover

Careful-Study-like shape: source consumed, draw two, discard two.

Absent graveyard-use permissions:

- source: `-1`;
- drawn identities: `+2`;
- discarded identities: `-2`;
- net quantity result: `-1` for the caster under the stated window;
- identity turnover: two identities entered and two different identities may have left.

**Required lesson:** quantity parity inside a sub-sequence is not identity parity. A CRD projection that only stores a scalar loses relevant resource-set truth.

### CRD-4 — Memory Jar / reversible intermediate state

Memory-Jar-like shape:

1. activating/sacrificing the source can remove the source identity from the controller's resource set;
2. each player temporarily exiles the current hand;
3. each player draws seven;
4. at the delayed cleanup, those newly held cards are discarded and the originally exiled identities return to hand.

**Required lesson:** report the intermediate resource profile separately from the final comparison window. Returning the original identities can reverse most temporary hand-set changes without resurrecting the consumed source.

### CRD-5 — Necropotence / delayed acquisition

Necropotence-like shape: a card is exiled face down now and is put into hand later at the specified end-step event.

- the face-down exiled card is not counted as a newly accessible card-origin resource merely because it is scheduled for later acquisition;
- record a pending/scheduled acquisition relation if represented;
- count the resource-set change when the later event actually moves the card into the accessible hand set.

**Required lesson:** information/state holding and future acquisition are not current access.

### CRD-6 — Teferi's Puzzle Box / count-neutral identity replacement

For a hand of size `N`, the hand identities are placed on the bottom of the library and `N` new cards are drawn.

- quantity delta may be zero;
- identity turnover can be `N` out / `N` in.

**Required lesson:** CRD must conserve identity-set change even when the compact count projection is parity.

### CRD-7 — Impulse / Sample Selection parity

Impulse-like shape: source spell is consumed; one selected card from the finite sample enters hand.

- source `-1`;
- selected identity `+1`;
- compact quantity result can be parity.

**Required lesson:** a semantically important retrieval operation can be CRD-neutral. Retrieval similarity and resource-count accounting remain separate layers.

### CRD-8 — Additional Execution

Flashback-, Retrace-, Rebound-, or similar execution opportunities do not mint a second card-origin resource merely because the same underlying card can be executed again.

**Required lesson:** Additional Execution is separately typed. A later execution may consume or move the underlying card, but the opportunity itself is not another CRD unit.

### CRD-9 — copies

Hive-Mind-like spell copies and other non-card copies contribute **zero card-origin units merely by existing**.

Their effects can later cause ordinary resource changes; those later card-origin transitions are accounted normally.

### CRD-10 — negative controls

A conforming implementation MUST NOT:

- count a spell/card copy as a card-origin resource;
- count a generated token as a card-origin resource;
- count merely looking at or revealing a card as access;
- count a scheduled future acquisition as a current accessible resource;
- erase identity turnover because the scalar count is parity;
- add temporary/intermediate deltas together as though they were all durable final-state gains/losses;
- recenter every multiplayer result on the source controller.

## 2. Sample Selection — retrieval/UI fixture contract

Sample Selection remains the finite-library-sample signature:

> an effect exposes a finite sample from a library without searching the broader library, then one or more cards are selected from that sample for privileged destination or use.

Bounded boundary clarification:

> For the ordinary Sample Selection signature, the sample extent is fixed by a count, resource choice, state-derived quantity, or other non-content stopping expression before selection. Scanning until a card-content predicate succeeds is Library Traversal, not Sample Selection merely because the scan terminates after finitely many cards.

This clarification preserves the current Tutor and Library Traversal boundaries without inventing a numeric threshold.

### SS-1 — Impulse

- source: controller's library;
- sample: top four;
- selection: exactly one card;
- selected destination: hand;
- unselected disposition: bottom in any order;
- visibility: controller inspection;
- classification: **Sample Selection**; not Tutor; not Library Traversal.

### SS-2 — Dig Through Time

- sample: top seven;
- selection: two;
- selected destination: hand;
- remainder: bottom in any order;
- classification: **Sample Selection**.

Delve/payment facts are separate from the retrieval signature.

### SS-3 — Collected Company

- sample: top six;
- eligible set: creature cards satisfying the printed mana-value condition;
- selection: up to two;
- selected destination: battlefield;
- remainder: bottom in any order;
- classification: **Sample Selection + Direct Placement**.

The battlefield destination does not turn the retrieval operation into Tutor.

### SS-4 — Plunge into Darkness

- sample depth: finite but player/resource controlled by the life payment/choice;
- selection: one from that exposed sample;
- selected destination: hand;
- unselected disposition: exile;
- classification: **Sample Selection**.

**Required lesson:** `finite sample` does not mean fixed-size or small-N.

### SS-5 — Fact or Fiction

- sample: top five revealed;
- staged authority: opponent partitions; controller chooses a pile;
- selected output: chosen pile to hand;
- nonselected output: other pile to graveyard;
- visibility: public reveal;
- classification: **Sample Selection with staged decision authority**.

### SS-6 — Genesis Wave

- sample: top `X`;
- eligible set: permanent cards satisfying the printed mana-value condition;
- selection: any number of eligible cards;
- selected destination: battlefield;
- remainder: graveyard;
- classification: **Sample Selection + Direct Placement**.

### SS-7 — Winota, Joiner of Forces

- repeated independent samples: top six per qualifying trigger;
- selection: up to one eligible Human;
- selected destination/use: battlefield tapped and attacking;
- remainder: bottom in random order;
- classification: **repeated Sample Selection + Direct Placement**.

Repetition/frequency remains an explicit coordinate rather than a new family.

### SS-8 — Gonti / Thief-of-Sanity-like opponent-library samples

- source library/player must be preserved;
- selected card may move to face-down exile;
- selected output may receive a later cast/use permission;
- visibility must preserve who may inspect the face-down card;
- classification: **Sample Selection + Exile Access/Card Use Permission as actually granted**.

Selection into exile alone does not imply unrestricted access.

### SS-9 — Hideaway / linked later permission

Hideaway provides the finite-sample selection/exile operation. A later ability may grant permission involving the linked exiled card.

**Required lesson:** preserve the link between the selected object and the later permission. Do not bake every later linked permission into the keyword's sample-selection event.

### SS-10 — Tomorrow, Azami's Familiar / replacement lineage

When a draw would occur, the replacement effect substitutes an instruction that looks at a finite sample, puts one into hand, and places the rest on the bottom.

- child retrieval signature: Sample Selection is valid;
- outer event semantics: the replaced draw MUST NOT also survive as an independently completed draw event.

**Required lesson:** a child Sample Selection projection does not erase the event-replacement lineage that produced it.

## 3. Sample Selection negative controls

### Index

Inspection/reorder without a selected object receiving privileged destination/use: **NOT Sample Selection**.

### Lim-Dul's Vault

Repeated inspection windows and reordering with no selected object receiving the required privileged treatment: **NOT Sample Selection**.

### Enlightened Tutor

Broader-library rules search: **Tutor**, not Sample Selection.

### Ad Nauseam

Content/choice-driven sequential traversal and acquisition rather than one pre-exposed finite sample: **Library Traversal**, not Sample Selection.

### Cascade / Discover

Scan until a content-dependent stopping predicate identifies a candidate; optional cast/hand consequences do not retroactively make the traversal a Sample Selection event: **Library Traversal**, not Sample Selection by default.

### Abundance

Replacement event plus content-predicate traversal to the first matching category: preserve **replacement lineage + Library Traversal**; do not emit Sample Selection merely because a card is ultimately acquired.

### Goblin Charbelcher

Traversal to a stopping condition without the Sample Selection acquisition signature: **NOT Sample Selection**.

### Non-library finite zones

Choosing from a graveyard, exile set, or other finite zone is not automatically Sample Selection. The current S16B signature is library-sample-specific; represent the actual selection/use permission instead.

## 4. UI/retrieval expectations

A retrieval/UI implementation consuming this fixture family should:

- rank hard operation signatures before the broad `Card Filtering` umbrella;
- distinguish Sample Selection from Tutor and Library Traversal;
- expose meaningful coordinates such as sample depth, chooser, eligibility, selection count, destination/use, nonselected disposition, source player, visibility, and repetition;
- permit compositional matches such as `Sample Selection + Direct Placement` or `Sample Selection + Exile Access` without creating compound ontology families;
- avoid treating generic Filtering overlap alone as strong Searcher B similarity.
