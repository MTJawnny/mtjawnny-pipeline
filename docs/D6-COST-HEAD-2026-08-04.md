## 1. The defect

```python
[{}]|\bsacrifice\b|\bdiscard\b|\bpay\b|\btap\b|\bexile\b|\bremove\b
```

The activated-ability test required a mana symbol or one of **six hand-listed
verbs** left of the colon, so every activated ability whose cost is an ordinary
action was lost:

| card | cost head |
|---|---|
| Barrenton Medic · Wall of Roots | `Put a -1/-1 counter on this creature:` |
| Quirion Ranger · Wirewood Symbiote | `Return a Forest you control to its owner's hand:` |

## 2. The work order's proposed fix was MEASURED and is insufficient

The work order said: *"CR 701's keyword-action list is ALREADY DERIVED in this
same file — reuse it rather than extending the hand-list."* Tested against the
27 candidates:

| | |
|---|--:|
| candidates | 27 |
| caught by the derived CR 701 keyword-action list | **11** |
| **missed** | **16** |

`return` and `put` are **not CR keyword actions** — they are ordinary English
verbs. Swapping the six-verb list for a 67-verb derived list would have been
**a hand-list wearing a derivation's clothes**, and it would have failed on the
majority of the very population it was prescribed for.

## 3. The CR publishes no cost-verb list — it publishes the STRUCTURE

> **CR 118.1** — a cost is *"an action or payment necessary to take another
> action or to stop another action from taking place."*

Deliberately open-ended. **There is no closed list to derive**, which is exactly
why every hand-list at this site fails. What the CR *does* publish is the shape:

> **CR 113.3b** — *"Activated abilities have a cost and an effect. They are
> written as **'[Cost]: [Effect.]'**"*

**So the colon is the claim.** The test is now structural, with two positional
guards.

### 3a. Measured safe before it was written

The risk of a pure-structure test is that it steals lines from other tokens.
Measured across the whole corpus — lines with a colon outside quotes whose head
misses the old verb list and which are **already on some other token**:

| token at risk | lines |
|---|--:|
| `loyalty` | 909 |
| everything else | **0** |

And the loyalty branch runs **before** this one (CR 606.1, the 2026-08-04 fix
that hoisted it out of the cost gate), so it claims all 909 first. **Nothing
else in the corpus was at risk.**

### 3b. The second guard — a colon inside the card's own NAME

`in_card_name(line, pos, card)` — a positional guard, the same shape as
`in_created_ability`. **Ultimate Magic: Meteor** is the case the work order
predicted:

```
Ultimate Magic: Meteor deals 7 damage to each creature.
```

That is a Sorcery's spell ability, not an `Ultimate Magic:` cost. It remains
`spell-or-static` after this change, verified.

**Root cause, reported not patched:** `fc.canonicalize_self_reference` should
have collapsed the name to `~` long before the cost test ran, and does not,
because the colon breaks its matching. That helper is **shared and ratified**
(DET preprocessing standard v1), so it is logged here rather than changed as a
rider on this pass.

## 7. What this item proves

**"Derive it from the CR" is not the same as "find a CR list to copy."** The
locked rule's point is that a hand-written stand-in for a *published closed
list* is a defect. Here the CR publishes **no** list — it publishes a
**grammar** — and the correct derivation was the structure plus two positional
guards. Reaching for the nearest CR enumeration because it was already parsed
would have been the cargo-cult version of the rule, and the measurement said so
before a line moved.
