## Reading notes

- In every row, an earlier clause marks one target object: gain control, pump, untap, put a counter on, attach an Equipment to, damage, or prevent damage to. A later "it", "that creature" or "that artifact" names that same object. Under CR 608.2c and 115.1 the reference is the target, and each recorded endpoint is that target's mark.
- Seven rows take the reference inside the mark's own clause: remove it from combat, gain control and it gains haste, the same-name comparison (2 rows), the blocking-creature power swap, the counter-and-flying row, and the damage-then-loses-flying row. In each one the pronoun names the target in that clause, so the mark is the right endpoint.
- Rows that name a second target (fight, deal damage equal to its power) were checked. The reference is the subject that acts, which is the first mark. The later target is the recipient and is not the referent.
- "another target" and "up to one target" marks (attack triggers, the modular-style move) are the referent of the following "that creature" or "that artifact". It is not the attacking source, which the card calls this creature or this land.
- Kicked replacements ("instead that creature"), conditional extras ("If you do", "Otherwise") and delayed triggers or end-step actions keep the same target. The target is chosen on cast even when its use is conditional (CR 601.2c), and a delayed trigger follows the same object (CR 603.7c).
